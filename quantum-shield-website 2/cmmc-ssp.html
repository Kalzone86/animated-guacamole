What is a System Security Plan (SSP) for CMMC?

What goes in an SSP, how assessors use it, and how to avoid the most common mistakes.

Quick answer: A System Security Plan (SSP) is the document that describes your CUI environment and explains how you meet each of the 110 NIST SP 800-171 requirements. It's required for CMMC Level 2, and it's the first thing an assessor will review.

Where the requirement comes from

NIST SP 800-171 requirement 3.12.4 (CA.L2-3.12.4 in CMMC) requires you to develop, document and periodically update a system security plan that describes your system boundaries, environments of operation, how security requirements are implemented, and the relationships with or connections to other systems.

What a strong SSP includes

  • Scope and boundary. What's in and out of the assessment, with a network or data-flow diagram showing where CUI is stored, processed and transmitted.
  • Asset inventory by CMMC category. CUI assets, Security Protection Assets, Contractor Risk Managed Assets, Specialized Assets, and out-of-scope assets.
  • Environment description. Locations, on-premises systems, cloud services and external service providers.
  • An implementation statement for every requirement. Specific, verifiable descriptions of what you actually do, who does it and which tools you use, not a restatement of the requirement.
  • Shared and inherited responsibilities. Which controls your cloud or managed service providers cover, backed by their customer responsibility matrix.
  • Roles and connections. Who is responsible for security, and how the in-scope system connects to other systems.
  • Version history. When the plan was reviewed and updated.

SSP vs. POA&M

The SSP describes how requirements are met today. A Plan of Action and Milestones (POA&M, requirement 3.12.2) lists the requirements that aren't met yet and how and when you'll fix them. Under CMMC, a POA&M is allowed only in limited cases at Level 2, so the goal is a plan with as few open items as possible.

Common SSP mistakes

  • Template copy-and-paste. Generic language that doesn't match how your company actually operates.
  • A plan that doesn't match reality. Assessors compare the SSP against interviews, configurations and evidence. Discrepancies cause findings.
  • No clear boundary. A vague scope makes everything in the company look in scope.
  • Missing provider responsibilities. Assuming a cloud provider covers a control without documenting it.
  • Written once, never updated. The SSP must change when your systems change.

How assessors use your SSP

During a Level 2 certification assessment, the C3PAO uses the SSP to understand your environment, confirm the scope, and plan which evidence to examine and whom to interview. A clear, accurate SSP makes the assessment faster and reduces surprises.
